The past few months have been very interesting (and scary) to track as a graduating economics major. Here is my perspective:

## Iran:

This has been a troubling time especially with the ever-rising gasoline prices as this conflict continues to wage on with seemingly no end in sight.

I have come to know oil as the lifeblood of trade, and rising oil barrel prices will inevitably lead to higher prices for everything else. This is nothing new, as Americans have seen high rates of inflation due to low federal interest rates. We never really "tightened the belt" after experiencing positive economic growth. Things might end up pretty bad for the average consumer.

## Employment:

There has been many issues with our employment data over the last year, but the consistent theme is that job growth has been much lower than in previous years. A lot of this can be attributed to the AI boom in the last three years that vastly impacted those who work in tech, but I believe that everyone is going to feel similar negative returns in the coming years.

We also have seen tens of thousands of layoffs from giant companies including Amazon and Oracle, as well as federal employees. This makes the job market even more difficult for the college new grad that now has to compete with people who have more years of experience in the workforce. College-grad underemployment rates and unemployment rates are rising, and verifiable numbers on this unemployment are becoming harder to find. Connections have always been important for upward socioeconomic mobility, and are even more important now in this "new world" (as my former gymnastics coach would say)

## Why this isn't seen as more important:

For the past year, I have been wondering why these obvious issues haven't been viewed as something to worry about. I believe that the stock market has given many people rose-tinted glasses as to how the economy is doing. I have always found this frustrating because the stock market is not the economy, but I digress. You can go on Twitter/X, see quote tweets on whether the market has gained or loss some ridiculous figure, and immediately get someone's opinion of government/company policy. I do understand that social media is not real life, but even with the bots, Twitter might be the "best" option one has to gauge sentiments on how things are doing. I do not know if or when the veil will lift, but these next few months and years will be interesting as I treverse this new world as a "recent graduate".
